# Does Prototype 2 encode Marcus's own causal model? A skeptic's evaluation

Reviewed: `main` at `cd79f1d` (PRs #11, #12 and #13 merged), the real Meditations build in `data/mirror/meditations` on the user's machine (built 2026-10-16, 30 passages), the offline planted-model benchmark, and three adversarial versions of that benchmark I wrote for this review ([skeptic-experiments/adversarial.py](skeptic-experiments/adversarial.py)). No Claude calls were made. Every number below is from code I ran or from files in the build, unless it is marked as inferred.

## Verdict

No, not yet. What Prototype 2 shows is that **when the data is generated the way PC assumes, PC can recover a graph.** It does not yet show that the graph it draws from the Meditations is Marcus's model and not something else. There are four other things it could be:

1. **The genre of the text.** The Meditations is mostly exhortation and portraits of good men. The situations pass codes them as situations where every virtue is high and every vice is low. The resulting associations come from praise, not from Marcus's beliefs about causes.
2. **How often Marcus repeats himself.** Each restatement of a maxim counts as an independent observation, so a link's significance measures repetition, not belief.
3. **Claude's own world model.** It enters through the reading step ("plainly shows"), the fill step and the vignettes. None of the existing checks can tell Marcus and Claude apart, and in the one real validation run they scored the same.
4. **Nothing at all.** At the corpus's real density, the tests that would separate a direct link from a mediated one almost never have enough rows. In the real build every pair was untestable and the graph was empty.

The orientation of every arrow comes either from Marcus's explicit claims (pins) or from simulated vignettes. Discovery from the corpus contributes, at most, a filter on which pairs are linked. So the honest description of the current causal tab is "Marcus's stated claims, filtered by a co-occurrence test of unknown validity."

## What "encodes the subject's causal knowledge" would have to mean

I would accept the claim if the system passed three tests. None of them is run today.

- **Content.** The edges match the causal judgments Marcus actually makes (his conditionals, his "take away X and Y goes", his "not X but Y"), on passages held out from the build.
- **Specificity.** Where Marcus's model differs from an ordinary modern reader's model or from Claude's, the Mirror sides with Marcus, and it does so more often than plain Claude asked to "think like Marcus".
- **Counterfactual dependence (the transplant test).** If the corpus is edited so that it asserts a different model, the output changes to match. If it doesn't change, the output came from somewhere other than the corpus.

## The case against, with evidence

### 1. The corpus is not a sample from Marcus's model, and the design treats it as if it were

PC assumes rows are independent draws from one joint distribution generated by the DAG. A row here is a situation Marcus chose to write about, in the form he chose: a prescription, a portrait, a memory, a maxim. The frequency of "mildness high, anger low" in the table measures how often he exhorts himself to be mild. It does not measure how mildness and anger co-vary in the world as he models it.

From the real build (83 situations from 30 passages):

| Situation kind | Count | Share |
|---|---|---|
| ideal ("the state the passage urges", per the prompt in `coding.py` `SITUATION_SYSTEM`) | 30 | 36% |
| actual | 21 | 25% |
| general | 21 | 25% |
| hypothetical | 11 | 13% |

Discovery ignores `kind` entirely (`causal/__init__.py` never reads it). So more than a third of the "observational data" is prescriptions. In a prescription every urged virtue is high and every urged vice is low by construction.

Repetition makes this worse. The Meditations restates the same handful of doctrines dozens of times. Each restatement is a new row, so p-values shrink with repetition, and the block bootstrap (blocks of 5 consecutive situations) does not remove that dependence.

### 2. The levels are valence, not states

- Of the 230 stated cells in the real build, **3 (1.3%) are "typical"**. The rest are ±1. The coder is answering "is this affirmed or denied?", which matches the first-pass coding prompt's own definition: level 1 means "present, affirmed, high or *to be had*" and -1 means "absent, negated, low or *to be got rid of*".
- **80% of stated cells sit at their variable's majority pole**, or 72% leave-one-out. A predictor that knows only "virtues are high, vices are low" gets 7 of every 10 cells right with no causal knowledge at all.
- Typical rows look like `b01s006:1` ("Sextus's character"): 12 variables, every virtue +1 and every vice −1.

**Experiment A (valence null).** This is a corpus with no causation. Each passage is an exemplary portrait, a cautionary portrait or neutral. A portrait puts each mentioned variable at its approved pole 80% of the time, with ±1 levels and the same topic-driven mentions as the benchmark. Results with the current pipeline, grouped topic included, averaged over 3 seeds at n = 600:

| Share of passages that are portraits | False links | 
|---|---|
| 0% | 0.7 |
| 30% | 6.3 |
| 60% | 8.3 |

That is worse than the topic-shifts-levels null the benchmark already reports (5 links). Topic conditioning cannot help, because valence varies within a topic. The real corpus is 36% "ideal" before counting the portraits coded as "actual", so I infer the real Meditations sits in the bad end of this range.

### 3. At the real density, PC cannot do the tests that make it PC

- **Real build:** 7 variables selected out of 60 candidates. All 21 pairs were removed as *untestable*, and the most rows on which any two variables were both known was 9. The test needs 10 rows per degree of freedom, and a 3×3 table has 4 degrees of freedom, so that is 40 rows. The class has size 1 and the graph is empty. Fill produced 0 cells and the vignettes had 0 targets, because targets require existing edges. So no part of the transformation has yet run on real data past validation.
- **Scaling up (inferred):** the full corpus is 412 passages × 2.8 situations per passage, which is about 1,150 situations, with about 2.8 stated variables each.

**Experiment B (planted model at real sparsity).** This uses perfect reading, the true topic structure and no reader error. Each situation states 2–3 variables:

| Situations | Edges | Skeleton precision | Skeleton recall | Arrows oriented |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 600 | 5.3 | 0.70 | 0.37 | 0 |
| 1,150 (about the full Meditations) | 13 | 0.64 | 0.83 | 0 |
| 3,000 | 19.3 | **0.46** | 0.90 | 0 |

Precision *falls* as data grows. The marginal tests gain power, but the conditional tests that would remove a mediated link (x–z given y) still don't have enough rows. Along the planted chains, those tests had 0, 16, 11 and 14 rows at n = 1,150, against about 120 needed. So more corpus makes the graph denser and wronger. Even under perfect reading, the full Meditations would give a graph in which roughly one link in three is false. None of its arrows would come from the data.

### 4. Arrows never come from the corpus

In every "situations" run of the benchmark, the planted model and both nulls, the data compels **0** arrows. All orientation comes from:

- **Pins from Marcus's stated claims.** These are legitimate, but they are text extraction, not discovery. With half the true arrows stated plus one false claim, the benchmark ends with 2.3 wrong arrows, because Meek's rules propagate the false one.
- **Vignettes**, which are Claude playing Marcus (section 6).

The CPDAG and the class-size counter are faithful machinery applied to a class that contains no orientations.

### 5. The richest causal evidence in the text is captured, then thrown away

- **Contrast pairs.** "Take away the opinion, and the complaint is taken away" is Marcus running an intervention in his own head. The situations pass records these: 19 of the 83 real situations carry a `contrast`. Discovery never reads that field. These pairs are the one source in the corpus that could orient edges *in Marcus's own model*.
- **Stated model claims are scarce and mostly off the variable list.** There are 13 `stated_model` entries in 30 passages, about things like `great_wealth → excess` and `moderation → pain`. Of the selected variables, only 2 claims hit a selected pair, and both are "untestable".

### 6. The subject transformation's checks can't tell Marcus from Claude

From `validation.json` in the real build:

- 3 held-out situations and 4 values. The subject transformation got 4 of 4. **Plain Claude, with no profile, also got 4 of 4.** The ordinary adult got 3 of 3.
- All 7 variables were marked **trusted**, 6 of them on 0 or 1 held-out values ("trusted on the overall score").

In the code (`subject.py` `validate`):

- A variable with 3 or more answers is trusted if its precision is ≥ 0.6. **It never has to beat plain Claude or the generic adult.** Given section 2, a valence-only predictor already reaches about 0.72.
- Precision counts only the answered values. A stand-in that abstains on hard cells looks better.
- The fallback trusts variables with **no** held-out data at all, on the overall score.
- The held-out task is "predict the hidden half of a situation's stated cells from the rest". Within a portrait or a prescription that just means predicting valence, so the test measures the confound from section 2.

In `leak_checks`:

- **Citation knockout** removes one cited passage and asks again. Marcus repeats himself, so a genuine view survives the knockout and gets flagged as a leak. A single unrepeated sample can also change for no reason. Without repeated sampling, an "unchanged" answer means neither prior nor subject.
- **The generic-adult diff** discounts exactly the places where Marcus agrees with common sense. That biases the model toward his contrarian views rather than measuring leakage.

### 7. Claude's priors can enter at the reading step, and nothing measures it

**Experiment C (reader leak).** On the planted corpus, the reader adds one unmentioned variable per situation with probability q, setting it from a folk model that differs from the subject's (an ordinary reader's "misfortune raises distress", "mortality raises distress"). That is a plausible reading of "plainly shows".

| q | Subject's edges found (of 10) | Folk-only edges found (of 8) |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 7.3 | 1.3 |
| 0.15 | 5.7 | 3.0 |
| 0.3 | 5.0 | 3.3 |

A modest leak replaces subject links with folk links, and everything downstream looks the same. The offline benchmark assumes perfect reading. The `--claude` benchmark has Claude both write and read the corpus, so the two sets of priors agree and reading accuracy is flattered.

### 8. Smaller problems

- **The "all" view manufactures edges out of defaults.** In the real build, 514 of its 581 cells for the selected variables (88%) are defaulted, against 67 stated or implied, and they turn an empty graph into 4 edges, one of them oriented (`distraction → discontent`). A constant fill correlates "was this stated" with level. That view should not be offered as a discovery result.
- **Vignettes in PC.** The context variable `do:X` is 1 only in vignette rows, so any general shift between Claude's vignette answers and the corpus situations (complete cells versus sparse ones, different marginals) loads onto `do:X → Y`. The within-vignette response test is sound, but the JCI run needs a pure "vignette origin" context or control vignettes.
- **The benchmark is built to agree with the method.** The planted mental model is a DAG that generates situation states, the nulls are the two confounders the method was designed for, the vignettes come from a perfect stand-in, and the data is denser than the real corpus. It is a good unit test. It is not evidence about Marcus.

### What Prototype 2 does get right

Several decisions are correct and should carry over:

- unknown is never coded 0;
- every cell has an evidence tier;
- every edge and cell keeps its provenance;
- pins can only orient, never add links;
- vignette contributions are drawn in a separate colour;
- an "untestable" result is reported as untestable rather than as independence.

The problem is not the machinery. It is what the machinery is pointed at.

## Recommendations for Prototype 3

Ordered by how much each one changes whether the claim can be true.

### R1. Change the data: from co-occurrence rows to the subject's causal judgments

Make the unit a **judgment**: one assertion by the subject about how things depend on each other, with its passage, wording and modality. The types are:

- **dependence**: "from x comes y"
- **intervention**: "take away x and y goes", "if you remove x"
- **screening**: "not x but y", "x harms only through y"
- **independence**: "whether x or not"
- **means–end**: "do x so that y"
- **contrast pairs**: already extracted

Fit the graph as **the structures most consistent with the weighted judgments**: constraint optimisation over ancestral and (in)dependence constraints, in the style of Hyttinen, Eberhardt and Järvisalo's ASP-based causal discovery and Triantafillou and Tsamardinos's work on combining independence constraints from many sources. Count weights over *distinct passages*, so that repeating a maxim is not new evidence. Report the equivalence class *of the judgments*. That class is a real object: it is the set of models Marcus's own words cannot tell apart.

Co-occurrence PC becomes a secondary check ("does the descriptive record contradict this judgment?"), not the generator.

### R2. Treat contrast pairs and conditionals as the subject's interventions

Each contrast pair is a do-experiment the subject ran in thought: the variable that differs between the two situations is the target, and the variables that change with it are its effects in his model. This is the only corpus-internal source of orientation that is Marcus's and not Claude's. Feed these to the fit as interventional constraints (JCI with "subject thought experiment" as the context), and draw them in their own colour, as the vignettes are.

### R3. Separate prescription from description

Route `ideal` situations, and any cell whose evidence is an exhortation, to a **values layer** (what Marcus wants to be the case), and keep them out of the causal layer. Replace valence coding with state coding: the reader must say whether the passage *describes* the level ("he was angry"), *urges* it ("be free of anger") or *evaluates* it ("anger is a vice"). Only description, plus conditionals and contrasts, goes to the causal layer.

### R4. Add valence to the confounder controls and the nulls

Until R1 to R3 land, give each situation a frame (exemplary, cautionary or neutral), conditioned on as a first-tier background variable like topic. Add Experiment A to the benchmark's nulls. A method that leaves more than about one false link on the valence null at the corpus's real density should not ship a graph.

### R5. Validation that can fail: specificity against plain Claude

Rebuild `validate` around three changes:

- **Questions from the subject's own causal judgments.** Hold out a set of conditionals and contrast pairs, and ask the counterfactual: "If the opinion is taken away, what happens to the complaint?"
- **Four stand-ins.** Marcus-transform, plain Claude asked to imitate Marcus, a generic adult, and an *opposed subject*: the same transformation built on a different corpus, such as Epicurus or the FOMC.
- **A trust rule that requires beating plain Claude.** Answer accuracy with abstentions counted as wrong, and a margin with a confidence interval over at least 40 items, not 4.

If Marcus-transform does not beat plain Claude, the profile adds nothing, and the honest outcome is to say so.

### R6. The decisive test: corpus transplant

Make a **"Marcus-prime" corpus**: the real Meditations with a minority of passages edited to assert a model that contradicts both Marcus and common sense (for example, "keeping death in mind breeds distress"). Rebuild, then check whether the reading, the fill, the vignettes and the graph follow the edit. Measure the **transplant rate**: the share of edited beliefs the Mirror adopts. A low rate means the Mirror is reporting Claude's Marcus, not the corpus's Marcus. This is the test that settles the question this review asks. Run it on FOMC too, with edited statements.

### R7. Benchmark v3: plant rhetoric, not just a world

Planted subjects should *write like people*: portraits, exhortations, maxims repeated at Zipf frequencies, contrasts, and a planted model that **disagrees with common sense** so leakage shows. Read the passages with real Claude, but with a different writer model than reader, or human-written seeds. Report everything at the real density: about 2.8 stated variables per situation and about 1,150 situations. Score against the planted *judgments* (R1) as well as the skeleton.

### R8. If co-occurrence discovery stays, make it honest about power

- Use fewer, coarser variables: 8–12, binary (the data is ±1 anyway), so tests need 10 rows instead of 40.
- Show an edge as "linked" only if its key conditional tests were testable. Otherwise label it **"unresolved: could be direct or mediated"**.
- Move to FCI. Valence, topic and selection are all latent or selection variables, and a PAG says so.
- Drop the defaulted cells from every discovery view.

### R9. Make every edge show where it came from

In the causal tab, mark each edge's evidence as one of:

- stated judgment
- subject's thought experiment
- co-occurrence only
- vignette (Claude-as-subject)

Add a flag for **"plain Claude draws this too"**. An edge whose only support is co-occurrence plus a plain-Claude match is the one least likely to be Marcus's.

## What to run next, on your machine

1. **The full 412-passage build.** The 30-passage build is too small to judge discovery, fill or vignettes. Prediction from Experiment B: an edge count in the low teens, 0 compelled arrows, and validation still unable to separate subject from plain.
2. **Validation at `n = 40`**, with the trust rule changed to "beats plain" (R5). This is a small code change.
3. **The transplant test (R6)**, on 20 edited passages. It costs one extra build.

If the full build passes R5 and R6, I'll withdraw most of this review. If it doesn't, the Prototype 3 graph should be built from R1 to R3.
